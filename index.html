Common questions

What is NHS Continuing Healthcare?

NHS Continuing Healthcare (CHC) is a package of ongoing care arranged and funded solely by the NHS for adults assessed as having a "primary health need". If your relative qualifies, the NHS pays for their care, including care home fees. It is not means-tested: it depends on health needs, not savings.

How long do I have to appeal?

Under the NHS National Framework 2022, a request for a review of the eligibility decision should normally be made within 6 months of the date on the decision letter. Check your letter, because it explains the local process. If you're close to the deadline, start Stage 1 now so there's time to prepare.

How long does an appeal take?

A Local Resolution appeal typically takes 3 to 6 months from submission until the ICB holds a meeting and issues a decision. If the case goes on to an Independent Review Panel run by NHS England, that usually adds 6 to 9 months.

Can you guarantee funding?

No. Nobody can guarantee a CHC decision. What we do is make sure the evidence is organised, mapped to the right domains and argued clearly, so the decision is made on the full picture.

What happens to my relative's records?

Health records are special category data under UK GDPR. We use them only to prepare your case, we never sell them, and you can ask us to delete them. Read our privacy notice.
