When well-managed needs still count

By Daniel Wyatt, Daniel Wyatt t/a CastilliAI. Written in plain English and checked by him. Last reviewed 24 November 2026. This is general information for England, not legal or medical advice, and not an official NHS assessment.

Families often read an eligibility letter that plays down a need because the person currently looks stable: the skin is intact, meals are being managed, or behaviour is settled with a particular routine. The National Framework does not allow a need to be set aside just because it is being successfully managed.

What the 2022 Framework actually says

The current National Framework (July 2022, later corrected) puts this in paragraphs 162 to 166, and states the same principle earlier in the document. The wording is:

The decision-making rationale should not marginalise a need just because it is successfully managed: well-managed needs are still needs. Only where the successful management of a healthcare need has permanently reduced or removed an ongoing need, such that the active management of this need is reduced or no longer required, will this have a bearing on NHS Continuing Healthcare eligibility.

Some older commentary, including earlier versions of our own draft notes, cited paragraphs 142 to 144 for this point. Those numbers come from an earlier edition of the Framework. In the 2022 text, the well-managed needs paragraphs are 162 to 166. If you are writing to the ICB, quote the current wording and paragraph numbers.

A necessary caution

The official Decision Support Tool guidance repeats the same principle, then adds an important limit. It is not meant to be applied as if routine medication or everyday care were simply not there. For example, needs managed with medication may belong in the drug therapies and medication domain, and it would not be right to score the skin domain as if good continence care was not being provided.

In other words: do not ignore a need because it is well managed; and do not invent a higher score by pretending that the care already being given does not exist. The question is what need is still there, and what skill, frequency and risk are involved in meeting it.

What this can mean in a review

If a domain was scored low only because a symptom is currently controlled, it is fair to ask the ICB to look again at the underlying need, the consequences if the intervention stopped or was delayed, and the evidence in the daily records. Care charts, incident logs and medication records are often more useful here than a general impression that the person “is settled”.

This is general information. It does not mean a particular score is “unlawful”, and it does not decide eligibility. That remains a decision for the ICB, looking at the person's needs as a whole, including their nature, intensity, complexity and unpredictability.
