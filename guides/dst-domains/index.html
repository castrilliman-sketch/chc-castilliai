The 12 Decision Support Tool domains

By Daniel Wyatt, Daniel Wyatt t/a CastilliAI. Written in plain English and checked by him. Last reviewed 24 November 2026. This is general information for England, not legal or medical advice, and not an official NHS assessment.

The Decision Support Tool (DST) is the national document used in England to help practitioners apply the National Framework. It is not itself the assessment of needs, and it does not, on its own, decide eligibility. A multidisciplinary team completes it after a comprehensive assessment, and the integrated care board decides whether the person has a primary health need.

The twelve domains

The official tool groups needs into twelve domains:

  1. Behaviour
  2. Cognition
  3. Psychological and emotional needs
  4. Communication
  5. Mobility
  6. Nutrition (food and drink)
  7. Continence
  8. Skin (including tissue viability)
  9. Breathing
  10. Drug therapies and medication
  11. Altered states of consciousness
  12. Other significant care needs

Each domain is recorded at a level such as no needs, low, moderate, high, severe or, in some domains only, priority. Not every domain has a priority level. The official guidance says that is because needs in some domains are not treated as enough, on their own, to indicate a primary health need.

The twelfth domain is for significant needs that are not covered by the first eleven. It can be scored from low to severe. It should not be used to tilt the overall decision unfairly.

What the guidance says about a clear recommendation

Official DST guidance says a clear recommendation of eligibility would be expected where there is:

  • a Priority level in any one of the four domains that have that level: breathing, behaviour, drug therapies and medication, and altered states of consciousness; or
  • two or more Severe levels across the domains.

It also says that the following may indicate a primary health need, depending on the combination of needs: a Severe level combined with needs in several other domains, or several domains with high and/or moderate needs.

That is not a guarantee. The ICB still has to look at the person's needs as a whole, including their nature, intensity, complexity and unpredictability, and at the legal limits of what a local authority can provide. A diagnosis, or a word such as “severe” in a medical note, does not automatically mean the matching DST level should be selected. It is the descriptor that most closely fits the need and the support required that should be used.

Well-managed needs in the DST

The same official guidance repeats that well-managed needs are still needs, and points to paragraphs 162 to 166 of the National Framework. See our note on well-managed needs.
