Time limits for asking the ICB to review a decision

By Daniel Wyatt, Daniel Wyatt t/a CastilliAI. Written in plain English and checked by him. Last reviewed 24 November 2026. This is general information for England, not legal or medical advice, and not an official NHS assessment.

If you disagree with an eligibility decision after a full assessment, you can ask the integrated care board to review the case through its local resolution process. If you disagree with a Checklist decision not to go on to a full assessment, you can ask the ICB to look at that again. This is set out in the GOV.UK public information leaflet.

What the letter should tell you

The National Framework says the ICB must tell you the decision, the reasons, and the arrangements and timescales for asking for a review. Read the letter carefully and keep the envelope or email if you have them. The date on the written decision is the date most ICB processes use.

The common 6-month period

The Framework itself tells ICBs to publish a fair process with timescales, and points to NHS England for further operational guidance. In practice, ICB procedures across England commonly ask that a local resolution request is made within 6 months of the written decision. That 6-month figure also appears in older national timeframes introduced for these reviews.

Those same timeframes, and many ICB policies, allow a later request where there is a good reason for the delay and the relevant records can still be obtained. So it is not accurate to say that the file is always closed for good on the day the sixth month ends. It is accurate to say you should not wait if you can help it, and that you should follow the instructions on your letter.

What “local resolution” involves

Every ICB must have a published local resolution process. The Framework says concerns should first be discussed clearly between the ICB and the family, with a written summary for both sides, and that the process should be fair and timely. Many ICBs aim to finish a local review within three months of a complete request, though that can take longer if records are delayed.

Our Stage 2 service is a Local Resolution pack for this first formal appeal after a refusal. It does not replace the ICB's process, and we cannot guarantee the outcome.

If you still disagree: NHS England and the Ombudsman

If local resolution does not resolve the matter, you can apply to NHS England for an independent review of the eligibility decision, or of the procedure the ICB followed. NHS England may first ask the ICB to try further local resolution. NHS England's public guide explains how to apply; ICBs often say a request should be made within 6 months of the local resolution outcome letter, and NHS England asks for its application form back within six weeks.

An independent review panel makes a recommendation. If you remain dissatisfied after that review, you can complain to the Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman. The Ombudsman process is about the NHS, not about this advocacy service.
